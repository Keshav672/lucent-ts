# Final polish pass

Runs on everything before I see it: every script, every variation, every hook.

## Build, in order
1. **Open loops** — open as many strong ones as make sense. End sections on the question, not the answer. Pay every one off. First 3 seconds carry a loop; it closes in the last 3.
2. **Intrigue upgrade** — swap flat constructions for specific ones with a planted origin, place, time or witness. "My professor told me" → "It all stems back to when my professor in college told me…" Specificity and loops, not hype adjectives. Understatement stays more credible than a rave.
3. **Value words** — answer more of the buyer's four unspoken questions (credit Kyle Milligan):
   - NEW/ONLY "Is this new, or the same stuff I tried?"
   - EASY/ANYBODY "Can I actually do this?"
   - SAFE/PREDICTABLE "What if it doesn't work for me?"
   - BIG/FAST "Will this actually change anything?"
   Lean on the axes the product truly wins (recorded in `brand/product-facts.md`); don't claim the ones it doesn't.
4. **VOC assembly** — pull every verbatim line the piece can carry, then lock to ONE speaker's register. No "y'all" line next to a New York line. One narrator, one age, one way of talking, start to finish.

## Check
5. **The 13 questions**, answered explicitly: 1 who is this for (sub-avatar + beliefs) · 2 why them · 3 broad or targeted · 4 does the hook have a gap, emotion and high stakes · 5 mechanism · 6 authority · 7 is it visual · 8 is it falsifiable (can someone say it's true or false?) · 9 could nobody else write it (search the headline in quotes; if it returns results, it's been written) · 10 what is it actually communicating · 11 6th-grade reading level · 12 can it be condensed without losing meaning · 13 would you say this door-to-door (spoken, not written).
6. **Visual words lint** (credit Harry Dry) — flag every abstract verb, noun, adjective, adverb and cliché; rewrite concrete. Not "productive routines": "waking up at 6am to write".
7. **Pacing** — short spoken sentences (median ~9 words), keep natural fillers, a 2–4 word verdict after the demo.
8. **Phrasing-key sweep** — no right-hand-column words.
9. **Condense** (Q12) — only now cut what doesn't earn its place.
10. **Register check, last** — casual, maximum intrigue, but something you'd scroll past on Reddit and believe. If an earlier pass pushed a line past believable, pull it back.
