# Copywriting by section

Reference the craft by the part of the ad being written, not all at once. Duplicates are correct (stakes and authority serve every section). Section-specific skills stay put (how to write a bridge is never referenced for a hook).

## Foundation (once per ad)
avatar · one angle · market awareness · value words axis · product facts · objections to clear · pacing/register · phrasing key.

## Hook
hook must-haves (start in action, zero backstory) · targeted vs broad · promise vs open loop · open loops (juxtaposition, fear, pattern interrupt) · unaware openers · hook library in `references/`
**+ duplicated:** raise the stakes · authority · labels and customer language · show-don't-tell (words and visual) · metaphor.

## Bridge / body
One job: keep the attention the hook won; the bridge decides drop-off.
bridge types, problem-bridge-product, in-media-res · mechanism · objections
**+ duplicated:** stakes (escalate mid-body) · authority · show-don't-tell (the demo is the ad) · metaphor (make it simple, reframe why old solutions failed) · labels · don't close the loop yet.

## CTA
Only once the payoff is delivered and belief is established.
pitching, transition patterns, brand vs creator voice, urgent vs non-urgent
**+ duplicated:** authority · stakes (urgency, loss aversion, permanence) · price objections (never defend price) · value words (SAFE/PREDICTABLE is answered here).

## Formats
- **Native** — entry promise = hook; story spine + mechanism + proof stack = bridge; offer + repeated CTAs = CTA.
- **Static** — nearly all hook. Its job is the CLICK, not the sale; a CTA is usually absent and that's correct, unless it's bottom of funnel.
